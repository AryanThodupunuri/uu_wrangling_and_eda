## Executive Summary

UVA is doing okay but not great on international philanthropy — around the 68th percentile of all US institutions in total dollars, but 10th out of 11 comparable public flagships, and that gap has been widening rather than closing. The fix seems fairly specific though: UVA only has active donor relationships with 15 countries versus 20-40+ for peers, so there's real room to expand into places like China, Canada, Germany, Saudi Arabia, and Hong Kong where we currently sit at $0 while peer schools pull in millions. My recommendation is to stop treating this as "raise more money everywhere" and instead target a short list of whitespace countries plus more sponsored-research/contract relationships, since that's the gift type where we're most behind.

## Summary of Findings

Market-wide, giving is extremely lopsided — a handful of countries (Qatar, England, China, Saudi Arabia) account for most of the roughly $17B in total gifts, and a handful of schools (Cornell, Carnegie Mellon, Harvard, MIT, Texas A&M) capture most of the largest individual gifts. The country giving the most money (Qatar, ~$2.7B) isn't the same as the one giving the most gifts by count (England, 3,600+) — Qatar is a few enormous checks tied to research partnerships, while England is steady and high-volume. The single largest flow overall was Qatar to Cornell at just over $1 billion.

For UVA specifically, we've received about $22.2M across 98 gifts, comfortably above the median university but behind our peer group. Our giving isn't overly concentrated — our top country is only ~19% of our total (versus 81% for NC State) — so the issue isn't reliance on one donor, it's that we simply don't have enough countries in the mix. We're also behind peers in Contract-based giving (67% for UVA vs. 84-89% for Michigan, UT Austin, and UNC), suggesting we're under-leveraging sponsored research as a channel.

## Strategy for Growth

I'd prioritize the whitespace countries where peers see real giving but UVA has little to none: China, Canada, Germany, Saudi Arabia, Hong Kong, Denmark, Portugal, and Ireland, plus underweighted relationships in England and Japan. These are proven donor markets for US higher ed already, so it's less about convincing a new country to start giving and more about getting UVA a seat at a table that already exists. I'd start with China and Germany given their scale, pairing outreach with engineering, medicine, and data science programs that tend to attract international research funding.

I'd also push harder on contracts and sponsored partnerships rather than chasing individual donors. Working with the research office to identify faculty with existing international collaborators, and helping formalize those into larger institutional agreements, seems like the clearest path — a few well-placed anchor partnerships (at a realistic scale for UVA) could move the needle more than slowly accumulating small gifts.

## Conclusion

UVA is in a solid but not top-tier spot on foreign giving, and the widening peer gap is a bit concerning. Still, this looks like a fixable, well-defined problem rather than a fundamental weakness — we're just not present in enough countries and underweighted in contract partnerships. A focused push into the whitespace countries above, paired with growing sponsored research relationships, gives UVA a genuinely achievable path to closing a good chunk of that gap.
